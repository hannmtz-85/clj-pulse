# ADR-0029: The clinical workspace: a browser client of the api, with keycloak-js, re-frame and the pages of the mock-ups

- **Status:** accepted (the ten points of the Decision validated one by one by the project owner on 2026-10-25; Phase 8, step 2)
- **Date:** 2026-10-25

## Context

`spa` is the clinical workspace: a single-page application in ClojureScript for a practitioner (their panel, the chart of a patient, the inbox of alerts, requests and visits) and for the administrator (the same, over every patient, and the status of the scheduler's jobs). It is the third of the four ways the project signs a person in: `web` is a server-side client (ADR-0028), the scheduler and the api use client credentials (ADR-0021, ADR-0025), and `spa` is a **public client with the authorization-code flow and PKCE**, which the realm already has as `pulse-spa` (redirect addresses `http://localhost:3000/*`, public, standard flow, PKCE `S256` required). The owner approved its mock-ups on 2026-10-17 (ADR-0026): the panel, the chart of a patient, the inbox, the appointments, the form to confirm one, the jobs and the empty and error states. The api it calls is complete (Annex B), and its CORS allows `http://localhost:3000` (ADR-0018).

The plan asked for a spike of at most an hour before deciding. It ran on 2026-10-25 in a scratch project outside the repository, against the real Keycloak and the api of `bb dev lite`, in headless Chrome 155. What it showed:

- **The primary stack works with no plan B:** shadow-cljs 3.5.7, Reagent 2.0.1 on React 19.3.1, re-frame 1.4.7, reitit-frontend and reitit-malli 0.11.0 and keycloak-js 26.0.8 (the same line as the server) compile and run together, and `libs/ui` and the `.cljc` of `libs/fhir` (`measurement`, `ranges`, `appointment`, malli) compile in ClojureScript **as they are**, and answer in the browser what they answer on the JVM.
- **The sign-in works as the realm has it:** `login-required` with `pkceMethod: "S256"` and `checkLoginIframe: false` goes to the themed login page and back to the page asked for (`response_mode=fragment`: the code and the state come back after a `#`, which keycloak-js reads and removes). The token lives only inside the keycloak-js object: after signing in, `localStorage`, `sessionStorage` and `document.cookie` held no token and the console none. `updateToken(30)` before a call and a forced `updateToken(99999)` really renew it. A `fetch` with `Authorization: Bearer` reaches the api (the preflight 204, then 200).
- **The pages of `libs/ui` draw in Reagent, with two kinds of noise.** Every sequence of children (`for`) warns "Every element in a seq should have a unique :key" (from 18 to 172 warnings a page, on every page) and React refuses `:tabindex` ("Did you mean `tabIndex`?"). A function of about 40 lines that walks the hiccup once, giving each element of a sequence a `:key` by its position and renaming the few attributes React spells differently, took both to **zero on all seven pages of the workspace**, which were rendered from the real hiccup of the mock-ups (dumped from the JVM as EDN) and look like them. A click on a component's button and a controlled text field reach re-frame through the attributes the components pass on. A vector of vectors as the children of an element crashes ("Invalid arity: 0"), as the project's gotcha says.
- **A trap of the optimized build:** `tokenParsed.preferred_username` and `.realm_access` are properties that Closure does not know, and it **renames them**: in the release build the user's name and roles were `nil`, and only a compiler warning (`:infer-warning`) had said so. Reading the claims as data (`(js->clj (.-tokenParsed kc))` and string keys) fixed it. The methods of keycloak-js itself are safe. The release bundle is 908 KB, unzipped, with every library in it.
- **The tools:** with `:deps true` shadow-cljs needs `thheller/shadow-cljs` in `deps.edn` too, and on Windows it needs `powershell` on the `PATH` (from Git Bash it fails; from PowerShell with `C:\Windows\System32\WindowsPowerShell\v1.0` on the `PATH` it works). A cold compile is 75 to 105 s, a hot reload under a second, a release 45 to 90 s. `reitit.coercion.malli` is in `reitit-malli`, not in `reitit-frontend`. Closure warns "unreachable code" twice for `user-menu` in `libs/ui` (a `doseq` with `:when` and a `throw`).
- **re-frame-10x 1.12.3** has preloads for React 17 and 18 only. With the React 18 one the application runs with no error under React 19, but its panel could not be opened in headless Chrome. It is not needed.

Three things make `spa` more than the portal in another dress:

- **The browser holds the token.** There is no server of ours between the browser and the api, so whatever runs in the page can read the token (and the refresh token keycloak-js keeps with it). The defence is that nothing else runs in the page: a strict CSP, no inline script, no third-party script, and few dependencies.
- **Every screen is made of several calls, and a panel of patients is many.** The panel of the mock-up shows for each patient the worst of the latest readings, the latest reading, a 14-day trend, the active alerts and the next visit. No endpoint gives that (X-79); the summary of a patient alone is twelve FHIR queries.
- **The same pages must exist twice, as mock-up and as application,** and the owner reviewed the first. The portal solved it by making the mock-up a call of the served page's function (ADR-0028: "what is reviewed is what is served").

## Decision

**1. The sign-in is keycloak-js 26.0.8 as a re-frame effect; the token never enters the application's state.** `pulse.spa.auth` wraps the one `Keycloak` object: `init` with `login-required`, `pkceMethod "S256"` and `checkLoginIframe false`; a visitor is sent to the login page and comes back to the page they asked for (the redirect address is the current one). The `db` holds who the person is (`id`, `username`, `name`, roles) and never a token; the claims are read as data. The token is taken from the object at the moment of a call, after `updateToken(30)`, and never logged, put in an event, a subscription or a storage (a test greps the events and the console of the journey for `eyJ`). When it cannot be renewed (the SSO session ended, or Keycloak does not answer for good) the person is sent to sign in again; a 401 from the api has the same result once. Sign-out is `logout` with the address of the workspace as `redirectUri` (registered as a post-logout address in the realm). **Only a `practitioner` or a `clinic-admin` uses the workspace:** anyone else is told "This workspace is for the clinic" with a way out and a link to the patient portal, and nothing is asked of the api. This is for the person's sake; the api still decides (ADR-0012). The realm needs no change.

**2. The pages are the mock-ups' pages.** A page of the workspace is a pure function of data, `.cljc`, in `apps/spa/src/pulse/spa/pages/` (`content` of a map of the api's shapes, `links` and `handlers`, as the portal's: **a key that is not there is a control that is not there**), which the SPA's views call and `pulse.design.workspace` (Babashka) calls for the mock-ups. So `design` stops writing the workspace's pages by itself and a test holds the served page to the mock-up's, as the portal's tests do. A link is a plain `href` (reitit-frontend's history takes the click of a same-origin anchor); a command is a function in `handlers`. Every view of the SPA passes the hiccup through **`pulse.spa.hiccup/->reagent`**, the function of the spike (keys by position for each sequence of children; `:tabindex`, `:colspan` and the like renamed), the only place that knows that libs/ui was written for a server; a test renders every page through it and fails on a React warning. The presentation code the portal has and the workspace needs (`format`, `views`, `charts`, `refusal`) moves, **when a step first needs it**, to a new library `libs/view` (`pulse.view.*`, `.cljc`, depending on `libs/fhir` and `libs/ui`) and `apps/web` requires it from there, its tests being the proof that nothing changed. `format` is the one that changes: java.time on the JVM and, in ClojureScript, `Intl.DateTimeFormat` used only to *read* the parts of an instant in Europe/Madrid (`formatToParts`), with the text written by our own code from a table of months and days, so that both platforms write the same strings; a table of instants (the nights the clocks change, midnights, the last second of a month) is run on the JVM and on Node and must agree.

**3. The state is re-frame's, in layers, with the effects at the edge.** Events are pure functions that return effects as data (`reg-event-fx`); the effects are `::http`, `::auth`, `::navigate` and the timers, and nothing else touches the world. The `db` is plain data of the api's shapes, by feature (`:panel`, `:chart`, `:alerts`, `:appointments`, `:jobs`), each part with its own state (`:idle`, `:loading`, `:loaded`, `:failed` with the kind of failure) and never an empty list for a part that could not be loaded: a part that fails, or that the api names in `unavailable`, is a message in its place with a button to try again, and the words are the portal's for the same situations. Subscriptions are layered (the raw part, then the view model); what can be decided without the browser (the order of urgency of the panel, the sections of the appointments, the filters, which buttons an appointment has by `appointment/available-actions` for a `:clinician`, the window of a series) is `.cljc` and runs on the JVM and on Node. A view never dispatches while it renders: loading belongs to the router's controllers (point 5). No optimistic updates: a command is sent, the api answers, and the part is read again.

**4. The `:http` effect is `fetch` with an `AbortController`.** It takes `{:method :path :query :body :on-success :on-failure :id}` as data. It gets the token as in point 1, sends `X-Request-Id` (which the api logs and CORS exposes) and gives up after 6 seconds, like the portal's calls. Every answer comes back as one of: success with `{:status :body}`, a *refusal* the page can show (400, 409 and 422, with the api's own problem: the title, the detail and the `errors` by field, which the forms write beside the fields), or a *failure* of one kind (`:unreachable`, `:timeout`, `:server`, `:forbidden`, `:not-found`, `:unauthorized`, `:aborted`) that the code decides on and the person is told in words. A request with an `:id` cancels the one before it with the same `:id` (the latest wins), and the router's `:stop` cancels what its page began. The body of a response and the token are never logged. The calls that change something are `POST` and `PATCH`; the api's `409` for a status that no longer allows the action is read again, as the portal does.

**5. Routing is reitit-frontend in history mode, with controllers, and the routes are data with deny by default.** `/` (the panel), `/patients/:id`, `/alerts`, `/appointments`, `/appointments/:id/confirm`, `/jobs`, and a page for what is not there. A route says `:roles` (a set; none is a start-up error, as the api's `check-routes!`, and a test walks the table) and a controller whose `:start` dispatches the loads with the parameters coerced by malli and whose `:stop` cancels them; the navigation shows only what the person's roles allow (the jobs, only to the administrator) and a route they may not open is a page that says so. History mode needs the server to answer the same page for every path, which point 8 does. On each change of route the focus goes to the page's `h1`, the `document.title` is set and a live region says what is loading, because a single-page application loses what a page load gives a screen reader.

**6. The panel asks the api for the patients and then for each patient, from the browser, with a limit.** `GET /api/patients` (up to 100 of the scope: the api restricts to the practitioner's panel, and to all for the administrator), then the summary of each patient and the 14 days of the measurement their headline reading is of, and the inbox and the appointments once, with at most six requests at once and one deadline for the panel. The rows show a skeleton until their data are in; the panel is put in the order of urgency once, when every row has arrived or the deadline has passed (a row that did not arrive goes last and says so), so that rows do not jump while a person reads. The filters (all, with active alerts, away from normal, normal) and the search box work on the loaded rows. A panel of more than 100 says "Showing the first 100 of N patients" (X-24). **An endpoint that gives the panel in one request (X-79) is not part of Phase 8**: the cohort has six patients, the browser's fan-out keeps the api as it is and every row can fail alone, and the cost is written in the backlog item. The alert count of the navigation and the inbox are read again every 30 seconds while the page is visible (the rules run at that pace); nothing else polls.

**7. The charts are the shared SVG chart.** The chart of a patient is `pulse.ui.chart/line-chart` through `pulse.view.charts` (the portal's, moved), with its band, its thresholds and its table of the numbers, not Chart.js (which the plan listed): the owner approved this look in the mock-ups, the SVG is accessible by construction and tested, the web and the workspace draw the same chart, and a canvas library would add a dependency and a second way of drawing. A wide chart for a desk and a compact one for a tablet, as the portal does for the phone.

**8. The commands use the lifecycle before the api, and the api's words win.** The buttons of an appointment are `appointment/available-actions` for a `:clinician`; the form to confirm or move one checks the times with `appointment/change` before it sends them; the acknowledgement of an alert is a `POST` and an alert that is no longer active (409) is shown as already acknowledged. What the api refuses (400, 409, 422) is shown in its own sentences beside the fields (`pulse.view.refusal`, the portal's). A button that sends is disabled until the answer, so that a double click is not two commands (the api's commands are repeatable or refused with a 409 except recording a reading, which the workspace does not do: X-81).

**9. The build, the serving and the policy.** `apps/spa` is a deps.edn project with `shadow-cljs.edn` (`:deps true`, npm only), `package.json` with every version exact and `npm ci`, and the `.cljs` of the application; `bb spa` runs shadow-cljs's dev server on 3000 (hot reload, and re-frame-10x if it works, as an option), `bb spa:build` a release (`:optimizations :advanced`, hashed file names; **the build fails on any compiler warning, the inference ones included**, which is the trap above), `bb design:spa` writes what is generated and committed (`resources/public`: the stylesheet compiled from `libs/ui` and `apps/spa/src`, the font, the icon, `index.html`), as `bb design:web` does for the portal; the compiled JavaScript is not committed. The release is served by **a small Ring server of ours** (`pulse.spa.server`, `bb spa:serve`, on 3000: the files, the same `index.html` for any path that is not a file, and the headers from `pulse.platform.http.headers`), so that the policy is tested now and nginx of Phase 9 copies it from the same data. The policy: `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'self' <the api> <Keycloak>; object-src 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'none'; form-action 'none'` (the origins from configuration; `connect-src` has Keycloak because the code is exchanged by `fetch`; no `unsafe-eval` and no `unsafe-inline`), `nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: same-origin`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY` and no `Cache-Control: no-store` on the hashed files (the HTML is `no-cache`). The dev server cannot have the policy (its client needs `eval` and a WebSocket); the acceptance runs on the release.

**10. How it is tested.** (a) The `.cljc` (pages, view models, filters, ordering, the effects as data of the events) runs in Kaocha on the JVM, in the suite `:spa`, and on Node (`shadow-cljs` `:node-test`, `bb spa:test`, part of `bb check`). (b) The events and subscriptions run with `day8.re-frame/test` on Node with the `::http` and `::auth` effects replaced by test doubles that record what was asked; `->reagent` and the pages are rendered on Node too and any React warning fails. (c) The pages are held to the mock-ups', and every page of every state goes through `pulse.ui.test.a11y`, as the portal's. (d) The serving: headers on real responses, the fallback to `index.html`, a path traversal refused. (e) **The acceptance is `bb spa:journey`**: headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol (no dependencies), the real Keycloak, `bb dev lite` and the release served with the policy: a visitor is sent to sign in; the doctor sees her panel in the order of urgency, filters it, opens a chart, acknowledges an alert, confirms a request, moves the appointment; the administrator sees the jobs and a practitioner is not shown them; a patient is told the workspace is not for them; the token is in no storage and no console message; no violation of the policy, with a control that must be blocked; no horizontal scroll and no target under 24 px at 820 and 1280 px in both schemes; signing out leaves nothing. The spike's script (`check.mjs`) is the start of it.

## Alternatives considered

- **A server-side client for the workspace too (a BFF, as the portal).** It would keep the token out of the browser, and it is how ADR-0028 chose for the patients. Rejected here because the four kinds of application exist to show four OAuth2 flows, and a single-page application with a public client and PKCE is the one the market asks for; the cost is point 1's reliance on the CSP, written down.
- **`oidc-client-ts` or a flow of our own instead of keycloak-js.** The plan pins keycloak-js to the server's line, and the spike showed it does what the realm needs; the code that would replace it is the part most worth not writing twice (ADR-0028 wrote the server side because `ring-oauth2` did too little; keycloak-js does not).
- **Chart.js** (the plan's choice): see point 7.
- **The pages of the workspace written again in ClojureScript, apart from the mock-ups.** They would drift, and the owner reviewed the mock-ups; the portal's way costs a move of some namespaces and an adapter.
- **The panel in one request now (`include` on `GET /api/patients`, X-79).** It is the better design at scale and moves the fan-out and its deadline to the server (ADR-0013), but it is a new api contract with its own tests and ADR for a cohort of six; the browser's fan-out is honest, replaceable (the page is a function of data) and it is kept in the backlog.
- **Hash routing** (`#/patients/...`): no server fallback, but the sign-in's `response_mode=fragment` would share the hash with the router. History mode and a server that answers the page for every path are cleaner.
- **Optimistic updates, a client cache (`re-frame-http-fx`, `re-frame-async-flow`, `re-frame-query`).** Another layer to explain and to get wrong; the screens are read often and written rarely, and the api's `409` is the truth. The effect is 40 lines.
- **A date library for ClojureScript (`cljs-time`, `tick`, `js-joda`) instead of `Intl`.** More code in the bundle for one zone; `Intl` is in every browser and in Node, and the parity test is what makes it safe.
- **Code splitting by page.** The bundle is 908 KB unzipped with everything in it; splitting is a later optimization.

## Consequences

- The token is reachable by any script that runs in the page. The policy, the absence of third-party scripts and `npm ci` with exact versions are the defence, and the journey checks the policy with a control. A dependency of the workspace is therefore a decision, and the build lists them.
- The release is about 0.9 MB before compression, loaded once and cached by its hash.
- **The portal changes in the steps that move code to `libs/view`:** names of namespaces, nothing else, with its 2 100 tests as the proof. The mock-ups of the workspace become calls to `apps/spa`, as the portal's did.
- The build needs Node and the JDK together, and on Windows `powershell` on the `PATH` when run from Git Bash; the tasks of `bb` set it up, and `CLAUDE.md` says so.
- `bb check` grows by the Node tests; a machine without Node already cannot run `bb design:check`.
- re-frame-10x is optional and unverified under React 19; if it does not work in a later step, the application loses nothing.
- Steps of Phase 8, one commit each: (1) the spike, done, with this ADR as its record; (2) this ADR; (3) the skeleton: `apps/spa`, the build and its tasks, the server and the policy, the adapter, a page that says who is signed in; (4) the sign-in, the renewal, the role gate and the sign-out; (5) the panel; (6) the chart of a patient; (7) the inbox and the acknowledgement; (8) the appointments, confirming and moving, and the jobs; (9) the states, accessibility and `bb spa:journey`.
- New backlog items when the steps need them: the panel in one request keeps X-79; code splitting; re-frame-10x on React 19; the spike's finding that `libs/ui`'s `user-menu` makes Closure warn is fixed in step 3.

## Update, 2026-10-25 (step 3, the skeleton of `apps/spa`)

What was built is in `CLAUDE.md` (section `apps/spa`), the plan (step 3, with the numbers) and the README. What the ADR did not say, and the owner should know:

- **A minimal sign-in is part of the skeleton.** The ADR put the sign-in in step 4 and the plan's step 3 said "a page that says who is signed in", which needs one. It is the effect of point 1 with nothing else: `login-required`, PKCE `S256`, no hidden frame; the claims are read as data; the page shows the name, the username and the role. Step 4 adds the renewal before each call (with the `:http` effect), the role gate and signing out. Doing it now let the release be walked in a real browser **under its policy** (the exchange of the code is a `fetch` to Keycloak, which `connect-src` must name) and with the real Keycloak, for a practitioner, the administrator and a patient, with the controls that the policy must block.
- **The static files are in `apps/spa/public` and not in `resources/public`.** The first arrangement, as the portal's, made two `public/pulse.css` (and two icons and two fonts) on any classpath that has both applications, the tests and `bb dev` among them, and `bb check` failed the portal's tests for it: which file a request got depended on the order of the classpath, and the portal could have been served the workspace's stylesheet, which has no rule for its classes. A test (`classpath-test`) holds that nothing of the workspace is under `public` on a classpath. The server of the release reads a directory (`SPA_ROOT`), not the classpath, so it needs nothing from `resources`.
- **The build fails on any warning** by reading the summary of shadow-cljs (`0 warnings`), and on a summary that is not there; shadow-cljs has no option that does it. On Windows the task adds Windows PowerShell to the `PATH` when a shell lacks it (Git Bash did), since the Clojure CLI of this machine needs it.
- **A path may end in one slash** (`/patients/pt-ana/` is the page), a name with an extension never does, and two slashes are no name. Jetty answers 400 itself, before the handler, to the ambiguous encodings (`%2e%2e`, `%2f`).
- **`libs/ui`.** `workspace-shell` has `:menu-button?` and `:theme-button?` (true, so the mock-ups do not change), because the workspace leaves out the two buttons of the mock-up that do nothing: the one that would open the menu of a narrow screen (the mock-ups are for a tablet and a desk: X-111) and the one for the theme (X-112, as the portal's X-93). `user-menu` no longer makes Closure say "unreachable code". `libs/ui/test-support` has a `deps.edn` so that the workspace's tests take its helpers as a dependency.
- **The pages of step 3 are not in the mock-ups.** The wait for the sign-in, its failure and the page of the person who is signed in are made of the components of the mock-ups (the frame of the pages of Keycloak for the first two, the frame of the workspace for the third) and are replaced, the third by the panel, in step 5. **Words that no mock-up had, for the owner to approve:** "Signing in"; "We could not sign you in" with "The sign-in service did not answer. Try again in a moment." and the button "Try again" (it reloads the page); "You are signed in" with "Name", "Username" and "Role" (and "Not given" and "None" for what the identity provider does not have), "Practitioner", "Clinic administrator" and "Patient" as the roles are said; and, for a browser that cannot run the script, "The clinical workspace needs JavaScript. Turn it on in your browser and reload the page."
- **New backlog items:** X-111 (the workspace on a phone), X-112 (the theme), X-113 (re-frame-10x on React 19), X-114 (code splitting).
- **Not yet checked:** re-frame-10x (left out), the behaviour of the sign-in when Keycloak is down (the page of the failure is a pure page and is tested; it was not seen in a browser), and `bb spa:build` on a clean checkout (it needs `npm ci` in `apps/spa` first, which the task says).

## Update, 2026-10-26 (step 4: the `:http` effect, the renewal of the token, the role gate and signing out)

What was built is in `CLAUDE.md` (section `apps/spa`), the plan (step 4, with the numbers) and the README. What the ADR did not say, or says otherwise, and the owner should know:

- **A 401 is one renewal and one repeat, and not a sign-in.** Point 1 said that a 401 from the api has the same result as a token that cannot be renewed: the person is sent to sign in again. The owner chose another rule on 2026-10-26, and that is what is built. The answer 401 makes the effect renew the token (even if keycloak-js thinks it is still good: the clock of the browser and the api's can differ by more than the 30 seconds it allows for) and repeat the call once (a call that was refused for its token was not handled, so repeating a `POST` or a `PATCH` does no harm). A second 401 is a failure of kind `:unauthorized`, which is told to the call's `:on-failure` and to one event, `::events/unauthorized`, that puts the workspace in the status `:unauthorized`: the page "Your sign-in was not accepted", with a link to sign in again that the person follows. The only sign-in that is automatic is the one for a token that cannot be renewed at all (the session of Keycloak is over, or it refuses the refresh token): that is what the person would do next, and nothing loops, since they come back with fresh tokens. The reason for the change is that an api that refused every token of this client (a missing audience in the realm is how) would otherwise send the person to Keycloak and back for ever.
- **The effect** (`pulse.spa.fetch`, with its pure part in `pulse.spa.http`, `.cljc`). A call is `{:method :path :query :body :id :on-success :on-refusal :on-failure}`; what comes back is an event with a map added to it. The path is a **vector of segments** and never a text written by hand: each is encoded from its UTF-8 bytes (so a `+` of an offset is `%2B`, a slash in an id stays in its segment, and the JVM and the browser write the same text), and a segment that is empty, `.` or `..` is refused, since a browser takes dot segments out of an address however they are written and an id that is one would be a request to another place. The answers are a success (`{:status :body :request-id}`), a refusal (400, 409 and 422, with the api's own problem, told to `:on-refusal` or, when there is none, to `:on-failure` as `{:kind :refused}`) or a failure of a kind; the kinds are those of the ADR plus **`:unexpected`**, for a status that is no success and no refusal and none of the others, and for a success that is not JSON. The token goes in the `Authorization` header and nowhere else: not in an event, not in the console (the effect writes nothing there), not in a storage. The call carries no cookie (`credentials: "omit"`), does not follow a redirect (the token would go with it) and has six seconds from the moment it is made, the renewal and the repeat included, after which it is aborted. A call with an `:id` stops the one before it with that `:id`, and `::fetch/cancel` stops one by its `:id`; a call that was stopped tells nobody anything. **There is no timer**: the token is renewed when a call needs it (`updateToken(30)`), as the ADR said, and the PoC's timer that started when its module loaded is the thing to avoid. The environment of a call is a map (`request!`), so that the tests give it a server of their own and a token that is not Keycloak's.
- **The role gate** is `person/allowed?`: a practitioner or the administrator. A test holds those roles to the ones that the api lets read the inbox of alerts, and to the ones that see a panel of patients but for the rules engine, so that the two cannot drift. Anyone else (a patient, a person with no role of the application, a service account) is shown the page "This workspace is for the clinic", with a link to the patient portal (`goog-define portal-url`, `http://localhost:3300`; the realm registers it) and a button to sign out, and **nothing is asked of the api for them**. The two buttons wrap, since a patient is likely to be on a phone and two large buttons do not fit side by side there.
- **Signing out** is a button of the menu of the person (`libs/ui`'s `user-menu` gained an item with `:on-click`, a `<button type="button">`; an item is a link, a form or a button and never two, and a link that signs a person out is never made: any page could send them to it). ## Update, 2026-10-31 (step 7: the inbox of alerts and the acknowledgement)

What the step settled that the ADR did not say. The owner validated the plan of the step on 2026-10-31 with its three defaults: **the inbox does not change under the hand** (the beat looks again and the page says so, and the person decides when to see it), **a count that cannot be read is a question mark**, and **the chart of a patient acknowledges too**.

- **The api needed no change.** `GET /api/alerts` has `status`, `sort` and up to 100 a page, `POST /api/alerts/{id}/ack` answers 200 or 409, the CORS of the api lets a `POST` through, and `:alert/ack` is the practitioner's for their panel and the administrator's for all ([ADR-0016](0016-alerts-inbox-and-acknowledgement.md)). The workspace filters nothing itself, as in the chart (step 6): it asks and the api decides whose alerts they are.
- **The route and the beat.** `/alerts?show=active|acknowledged` (both when there is none) is a route of the table, whose new key `:refresh` says what the beat asks the page to do while the person is on it. The beat is `pulse.spa.watch` (pure) and `pulse.spa.watch-events`: every 30 seconds, while the tab is seen, it reads the count of the alerts that need a decision (`GET /api/alerts?status=active&limit=1`: the `total` is all that is read) and dispatches the `:refresh` events of the route. It is the first thing in the workspace that repeats, so the point of its design is that it cannot pile up: **one run at a time, with an epoch**, so the beat of a run that is over does nothing and arms nothing; a tab that is hidden asks for nothing and arms the next beat, and **the moment it is seen again a new run begins and asks at once** (one `visibilitychange` listener, made once); and a workspace that is no longer signed in (the api did not accept the sign-in) does not arm another. This also settles X-116 for the alerts. The ADR's "while the page is visible" is `document.visibilityState`, read by a `cofx`.
- **A count that could not be read is `?`.** The navigation shows the number of alerts that need a decision beside "Alerts" (none for zero, and none until the first answer), and **`?` when the api did not answer** (a screen reader hears "Alerts, could not be read"): a badge that went away would say that there are no alerts, which is the one thing a clinician must not be told by mistake. The count is read out of turn after every acknowledgement.
- **The inbox is a small machine** (`pulse.spa.inbox`, pure, the same kind as the panel's and the chart's): the active alerts and the acknowledged ones (100 each, the newest first, as the api sorts them), the patients (for the names: an alert names its patient by a reference) and, for each patient and each measurement that the rule of an active alert watches, the latest three readings behind it (`pulse.view.alerts`), at most six calls at once under one deadline, each part its own state and **never an empty list for one that failed** ("The alerts could not be loaded", with a way to try again, is not "No alerts"). An alert whose patient is not among the first hundred is named by their number ("Patient pt-ana"; X-130).
- **What is shown does not move on its own.** The ADR says that the inbox is read again every 30 seconds; the step settles *what happens to what is shown*. A list that rearranges itself as a hand goes to a button is how the wrong alert is acknowledged, and content that updates by itself must be under the person's control (WCAG 2.2.2). So the look that the beat asks for reads both lists again and **keeps what it finds apart**; if the alerts that need a decision are not the ones on the page (the set of ids, and how many there are in all), the page says **"The alerts have changed"** with a **"Show the changes"** button, and shows nothing new until it is pressed (it then asks for the readings of the alerts that are new, and puts the focus on the heading, since the button that was pressed is gone). A look that fails says "We could not check for new alerts" and leaves what is shown. **What the person did themselves is shown at once**: after an acknowledgement the inbox looks again and shows what it finds. The count of the navigation, which is a number and not a list, is live.
- **Acknowledging.** The button sends `POST /api/alerts/{id}/ack` and is busy (disabled, with a spinner, `aria-busy`) from the click until the api has answered **and the page has been read again**, so a second click is not a second command; there is no optimistic update (point 3).
