# ADR-0014: Recording and reading observations: a catalogue of measurements, one stored unit, a bounded series

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-14

## Context

`GET` and `POST /api/patients/{id}/observations` are the first endpoints that write clinical data and the first that return a time series. Four questions had no answer yet. What does a client send to record a reading: a FHIR Observation, or something smaller? Where are units settled, when patients type pounds, mmol/L or °F while the rules engine and the reference bands assume one unit each? How far is a number trusted, when a typo or a wrong unit must not become a data point that a rule then raises an alert on? How is a series asked for and bounded? And what is audited on a write?

## Decision

- **A catalogue of measurements, as data** (`pulse.fhir.measurement`, portable `.cljc`). Six names: `blood-pressure`, `heart-rate`, `spo2`, `glucose`, `weight` and `temperature`, the same as the signals of the demo cohort. Each says which catalogue concept (LOINC) it is, the one UCUM unit it is stored in, how many decimals a stored value keeps, its plausible limits and the reference band that classifies it. Blood pressure is a panel with two channels, systolic and diastolic; every other measurement has one, `value`. Clients speak in these names, not in LOINC codes or FHIR; the api builds the Observation.
- **Units are settled at the door.** A reading may arrive in any unit `pulse.fhir.units` can convert to the stored one (mmol/L for glucose, lb or g for weight, °F or K for temperature, kPa for pressure) and is stored in the stored unit, rounded to the measurement's precision: whole numbers for heart rate, pressure, glucose and SpO₂, one decimal for weight and temperature. Every series is therefore in one unit, and neither the rules nor the charts convert.
- **Two kinds of "no".** A body that does not have the shape of the endpoint (a missing field, a wrong type, an unknown measurement, a timestamp that does not parse) is a **400** from the coercion, with its humanized messages. A well-formed body that cannot be a measurement (a unit that measurement cannot have, a value outside the plausible limits, a diastolic not below the systolic, a time in the future or too far in the past) is a **422**, with the same `errors` member: field name to messages. The limits are wide on purpose (heart rate 20-300 /min, SpO₂ 50-100 %, glucose 10-1000 mg/dL, weight 1-500 kg, temperature 25-45 °C, systolic 40-300 and diastolic 20-200 mmHg). They are a net for typos and unit mix-ups and not clinical limits: unusual readings are what the app is for, and the reference bands and the rules deal with them.
- **The server decides what the client must not.** The subject is the patient in the path, so a body cannot name another; the performer is the caller's own FHIR resource (the patient who reports on themselves, the clinician who enters a reading, nobody for the rules engine, which has none); the status is `final`; the id is a new `obs-<uuid>`, written with PUT like every other resource. `at` defaults to now, is stored in UTC and must not be more than 5 minutes ahead of the server's clock (clock skew) nor more than 30 days back, so that a mistyped year does not rewrite a chart. Both limits are constants for now.
- **The patient must exist.** POST reads the patient before writing and answers 404 when there is none: HAPI would refuse a reference to a missing patient with a message the caller cannot act on, and the in-memory fake would accept it.
- **Created is 201 with the reading** (`id`, `measurement`, `at`, `value` or `systolic` and `diastolic`, `unit`, `band`, `by`) and no `Location`: the endpoint that would serve one reading is not in Annex B.
- **The series.** `GET ...?measurement=heart-rate&from=&to=&sort=&limit=`. `measurement` is required, because a series is of one thing. `from` is inclusive and `to` exclusive, each a date (midnight UTC) or an instant with its offset, in the years 1900 to 2199 (anything else is a mistake, and FHIR could not be asked about it); they are sent to FHIR as UTC instants, since HAPI reads a bare date in its own time zone. In a query string a `+` is a space, so an offset is written `%2B02:00`. Newest first unless `sort=date`; at most `limit` readings (default 100, at most 500), and `truncated` says that more matched. There are no offsets and no total: to go further back, ask again with `to` set to the `at` of the oldest reading received. An empty series is `200` with no items. A patient that does not exist is a 404 for staff, found out only when the series is empty (one extra read), and a 403 for a patient asking for another's, as everywhere (ADR-0012).
- **The reading is the summary's, with provenance.** `pulse.api.observations/reading-view` is what the summary already showed for a latest vital sign (value, unit, `at`, band, and for blood pressure the worse band of the two components); the series adds `measurement` and `by`, the performer, which tells a self-reported reading from one entered by staff. The summary now takes its vital signs and their concepts from the catalogue instead of a table of its own.
- **Audit.** The decision to record is audited as before (`:pulse.audit/access`). The write adds `:pulse.audit/write`, raised once the FHIR server has accepted it: who, the action, the patient and the reference of the new resource, never its value. The audit functions move from `authz` to `pulse.api.audit`, because appointments and alerts will write too.

## Alternatives considered

- **The body is a FHIR Observation.** No new vocabulary, but every client must know LOINC, UCUM and the categories, could name another subject or performer, and still could not be trusted on units or plausibility, which the server would check anyway. The api's own endpoints take the small shape; FHIR is what HAPI speaks.
- **Store the unit that was sent.** Nothing is converted or rounded, but then every consumer must convert, and one series can mix units.
- **Plausibility from the reference bands.** That conflates "unusual", which is the point of the app, with "impossible".
- **Offset paging with a total, as the panel does.** A series is already cut by a date range, which pages with no state on the server, and a total needs a count the FHIR client does not offer.
- **A deterministic id from patient, code and time**, which would make a retry harmless: but two different readings in the same second would silently replace one another. An idempotency key is a backlog item.

## Consequences

- A POST that is retried records the reading twice, and a reading cannot be corrected or withdrawn yet. Both are in the backlog (X-25, X-26).
- A new measurement is one entry in the catalogue; recording, the series and the summary know it at once. The scheduler's simulator and the SPA's form can reuse the catalogue, which is `.cljc`.
- Recording costs one Patient read, and a practitioner's request a second one, because the authorization step read it first (ADR-0012, X-24).
- The 30-day window binds the rules engine too: a simulator that wanted to backfill history would need the limit to be configurable, or to write to FHIR directly as the seed does.
- The reading is audited by reference, so the audit trail can say that Dr. Rivera recorded `Observation/obs-…` for `Patient/pt-ana` at a given time without ever holding a health value.
