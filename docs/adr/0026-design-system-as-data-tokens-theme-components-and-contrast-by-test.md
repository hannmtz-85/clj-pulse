# ADR-0026: The design system as data: tokens in EDN, a theme generated from them, shared hiccup components and contrast checked by a test

- **Status:** accepted (the approach was validated point by point by the project owner on 2026-10-16; Phase 6). At the first review, the same day, the owner approved the style tile and chose **teal**: see the update at the end of the decision.
- **Date:** 2026-10-16

## Context

Phase 6 gives the three interfaces one look: `web` (the patient portal, rendered on the server with Hiccup and htmx), `spa` (the clinical workspace, Reagent) and the login page of Keycloak (FreeMarker). The plan names the pieces (Tailwind 4 and daisyUI 5, a light and a dark theme, AA contrast, a sidebar and header layout, components, a chart style, navigable mock-ups and a final login theme) and one rule: the owner approves the mock-ups before any interface is written. Four things have to be decided:

- **Where the look is defined**, so that three technologies share it and a colour is changed in one place.
- **How the AA claim is made true, and stays true**, when every palette and both themes have to meet it.
- **How a component is written once** for a server that renders strings and a browser that renders React.
- **How the owner reviews it** without the work being done again after each remark.

## Decision

**The tokens are data.** `design/tokens.edn` holds the palettes (three candidates at first, teal, blue and indigo: the owner chooses one at the first review and the others are deleted), each with a light and a dark scheme; the neutrals; the tones of a reading's band (normal, and three degrees away from it, the four that `pulse.fhir.ranges/severity` has); the colours of a chart; the typography, radii and shadows; and the list of **pairs of colours that must meet a contrast**, with the ratio each needs: 4.5 for text and 3 for what a person has to see to use a control or to read a graphic (WCAG 2.2 AA).

**A theme is generated from them.** `tools/design.clj`, which is the same under Babashka and on the JVM, writes `design/theme.css`: the daisyUI themes (`@plugin "daisyui/theme"`), the custom properties `--pulse-*` for what daisyUI has no variable for (the tones of the bands, the border of a control, the colours of a chart), and the `@font-face` of the font. It is committed and checked by `bb design:check`, part of `bb check`, as the realm JSON is.

**Contrast is a test.** The ratio is the WCAG formula, a pure function. The test fails when any pair of any palette in either scheme is below its minimum, and the style tile prints the measured ratio beside each swatch, so that the owner sees the numbers and nobody has to take them on trust.

**Tailwind 4 and daisyUI 5 are built from npm.** `design/package.json` pins Tailwind CSS and its CLI, daisyUI, the Inter font (variable, OFL) and the Lucide icons (ISC), all for development; `node_modules` is ignored by git and the lock file is committed. Tailwind reads the Clojure sources for class names, so **a class is written whole, as a string literal**: `"btn btn-primary"`, never `(str "btn-" kind)`, which it cannot see. The font is in the repository as files and the icons as Clojure data (`libs/ui`, generated by `bb design:icons`); no page asks a CDN for anything.

**Components are shared hiccup.** `libs/ui` (`.cljc`) has presentational functions, `(button {:variant :primary} "Save")`, that return hiccup data: no state and no handlers, and the attributes they are given go through, so the `hx-*` attributes of htmx and the `:on-click` of Reagent both work. They are rendered to strings by Hiccup 2 in `web` and by React in `spa`. A chart is a function from data to SVG (`pulse.ui.chart`), accessible by construction: a role, a title, a description and a table of the same numbers. Charts are drawn for a width: the portal draws a compact one and a wide one and shows the one that fits.

**The mock-ups are generated.** `bb design:build` renders the pages from the components, with real data from the synthetic cohort, to static HTML in `design/dist/` (ignored by git), with an index to move between them, and `bb design:shots` makes screenshots with headless Chrome so that they can be looked at without opening each. There are two points of review: first the style tile (the palettes, the type, every component and a chart, in light and dark), then the pages.

**The Keycloak login** is a theme of FreeMarker templates (planned over the `keycloak` theme and built over `base`, see the update of 2026-10-17): the frame and the pages that exist in our flows (sign-in, error, information, logout confirmation and expired session), with a stylesheet compiled from the same tokens. That CSS is committed in the theme, because the stack must start without Node.

**Accessibility** is WCAG 2.2 AA: the contrast by the test above; a visible focus ring that comes from a token; targets of at least 44 px in the portal; no meaning conveyed by colour alone (a band has an icon and a word, a series of a chart a dash and a marker); reduced motion respected. What a program can see in hiccup is checked by a test of its own, `pulse.ui.test.a11y`, which every component and every page of the mock-ups must pass (labels of controls, names of buttons and links, svg hidden or named, ids that exist and are unique, captions and scopes of tables, headings that do not skip a level, and for a page the language, the title, the viewport, one h1 and a main). Focus order, reflow and the size of targets are checked by hand until the extra X-32.

**Update, 2026-10-16: the first review.** The owner approved the style tile (the look, the components, the way a reading away from normal is shown) and chose teal. Blue and indigo were deleted from `design/tokens.edn` in a commit of their own, `:palette :selected` is `:teal`, and the themes are called `pulse` and `pulse-dark`; the generator still writes a theme for each of several palettes when none is selected, and the tests of that use the tokens of the review, made in the test. The same review decided that a patient can cancel their own appointment, which the plan and Annex A did not allow (the portal said "request/cancel" and Annex A gave the patient only the request): the mock-ups have the button, and the api gets the action in Phase 7, with its own ADR.

**Update, 2026-10-16: the mock-ups of the interfaces (the second review).** A mock-up of an interface is two documents. The interface itself is `frames/<page>.html`, with no trace of the review (no navigation between mock-ups, no choice of theme), and the page of the review has a frame around it that is as wide as a phone (390 px), a tablet (820) or a desk (1280), chosen with a button: the breakpoints of the page are those of the frame and not of the window it is looked at in, and the frame is as high as the page in it, which tells its height through `postMessage`, since a file is its own origin. There are 21 mock-ups: the sign-in and the other pages of Keycloak (two); the patient portal (home, readings, record a reading, appointments, the question before cancelling one, record, profile, a new patient, and what goes wrong); and the clinical workspace (the panel, the chart of each of the four patients of Dr. Elena Rivera, the alert inbox, requests and visits, confirming an appointment, the jobs for the administrator, and empty states and errors). The charts of three of the four patients are pages that the navigation does not list (`:listed? false`) and the panel links to. They are made from the cohort at its fixed moment and, for what the cohort does not hold (the alerts, the jobs, two appointments of Ana), from data written by hand in the shapes of the api and checked against its schemas. What a page offers is held to what the system can do: the buttons of an appointment come from `pulse.fhir.appointment/available-actions`, the limits of a reading and of a request from `measurement` and `appointment/limits`, the words of an alert from the rules (a test compares them), and the portal promises nothing the api does not have: no editing of the profile, no notifications by email, no "your care team has been told" (the portal can see the band of a reading and not the alerts), no visits that took place (the api knows `proposed`, `booked` and `cancelled`) and, in the inbox, nobody who acknowledged an alert, since the api does not say. The shells are in `libs/ui/layout`: the portal has a bar at the top from a medium screen and a dock at the bottom of a phone, with targets of 44 px or more (a test checks that every button of the portal is a large one); the workspace has a sidebar that is full from a large screen and a rail of icons from a medium one, in 14 px type, with a dot for the count of alerts on the rail, and the administrator has one more group, Administration, with the jobs. A narrow screen has its own rules: an alert puts its buttons under its text, a row of a list puts its chips and buttons under the text when they are wide (`:trailing-wraps?`), the buttons of a dialog are one above the other with the one that decides first, and a table hides a column (`:class` on a column) or shortens its heading. The new components are `stat`, `person`, `list-group` and `list-row`, `date-block`, `icon-disc`, `pill-nav`, `breadcrumbs`, `search-field`, `user-menu`, `dialog` and `logo`; a toast was made and deleted, because a message in the corner of the window cannot be seen in a frame that is as high as its page (X-85). The screenshots of the review are taken of the frames at the widths of each mock-up, and what they are is a pure function (`pulse.design.shotplan`) that a test holds. The pages found a pair that the first list of contrasts did not have: the brand as the text and the border of an outline button inside a message of a tone. It was added for the five tones (54 pairs now, 108 measurements), and on the light scheme the critical one was 4.48:1, so the soft background of the critical tone went from `#fee2e2` to `#fee7e7` (4.63:1; the ink on it went from 8.20 to 8.49).

**Update, 2026-10-17: the login theme of Keycloak.** The theme `pulse` extends `base`, not `keycloak`: `base` has the markup of every page Keycloak can show and no stylesheet, while `keycloak` brings a PatternFly stylesheet that a design of our own would have to fight. The templates are not written by hand: `pulse.design.keycloak` makes them from the components of `libs/ui` that the mock-ups are made of (`login-card`, `login-title`, `login-subtitle`, `login-info`, `login-frame`, the fields, the alert, the buttons), with raw FreeMarker in among the hiccup for what Keycloak fills in at run time (`ftl`, `msg`, `when-ftl` and `either-ftl`), so that a page of the real login is the page of the review: the same card, the same classes and the same English. `bb design:keycloak` writes `theme.properties`, the templates (`template.ftl`, the layout every page uses, and `login`, `error`, `info`, `logout-confirm` and `login-page-expired`, the pages the realm uses), `messages_en.properties` and the icon into `infra/keycloak/themes/pulse/login`, copies the fonts from `design/fonts`, and compiles `resources/pulse.css` with Tailwind from `design/css/keycloak.css`: the base rules and the theme of the interfaces, and only the classes the templates and `theme.properties` use (41 KB). The pages of `base` that the theme does not rewrite (a new password, a one-time code) keep their markup inside the frame of this theme, with the classes that `theme.properties` gives to `kcFormGroupClass`, `kcInputClass` and the others. The English is written once: `messages` makes `messages_en.properties` and the mock-ups read it (`pulse.design.keycloak/message`), together with `base-messages`, the few words of Keycloak's own that a template asks for and the theme leaves as they are. What differs from the mock-up the owner approved, and why: the line with "Keep me signed in" and "Forgot your password?" is not on the page, because the realm allows neither (a new password is sent by email and the stack has no mail server; the template shows the line when a realm allows them); the subtitle is the name of the client, "to continue to Pulse web portal" or "... Pulse API documentation (Swagger UI)", which Keycloak knows and the mock-up could only guess; the message after signing out is a card with its title and one way back to the application when Keycloak knows which; the expired page offers two ways, to start again and to go on; there is a fifth page, the error of a request Keycloak refuses; there is no toggle to show the password (it needs an icon font or a script) and no choice of language (the realm has one language: X-30).

**The login theme is tested in four ways**, because a template that Keycloak fills in at run time is the part of the system a program of ours never sees. (1) The files: a test fails when a committed file is not what the generator makes, or a file is in the theme that nothing makes, when a class the templates or the properties use has no rule in the committed stylesheet, when a colour of `design/theme.css` is not in it (run `bb design:keycloak` after a change of tokens), when a font is not the one of `design/fonts`, and for the messages, the properties, the icon, the realm (`loginTheme`) and the mount of `compose.yaml`. (2) The pages: FreeMarker 2.3.32, the version Keycloak 26.0.0 ships, is a dependency of the `:test` alias, and the real templates are filled in with the engine configured as Keycloak configures it (a `.ftl` is HTML, so what `${...}` writes is escaped and `?no_esc` lets a sanitized message through; `msg` is a MessageFormat over the messages and gives back the key it has no message for) and a model of what Keycloak gives a page. The HTML is read back into hiccup by `pulse.ui.test.html`, a small reader of the HTML that Hiccup and FreeMarker make, which fails on an element that is not closed or is closed out of order, and then put through `pulse.ui.test.a11y`, the checker the components and the mock-ups pass. The scenarios are each branch of each page (the label the realm chooses, a wrong password, what the realm allows, the second step, providers, every kind of message, the required actions, a page with no session at all), what a person typed or an administrator named is text and never markup, the text of a message goes through the sanitizer, and every message a template asks for was asked for by some scenario. The pages are the review's: the parts of the sign-in and of each of the other four cards (headings, labels, fields, buttons and links, with their classes and the attributes that decide how they work) and the words in the order they are read are those of the mock-up. (3) The tools: the sign-in of `bb token` and `bb realm:smoke` read the address of the form with `tools/loginpage.clj`, whatever the order of the attributes of the tag, because Hiccup writes them alphabetically and the regular expression of the first version needed `id` before `action`. (4) The real thing: `bb test:it` reads the sign-in, a wrong password, an error, the question before signing out and the expired page from the real Keycloak, runs the same checker on them, and fetches the stylesheet, the fonts and the icon that the pages ask for and compares them byte for byte with the committed files, which is how a stack that has an old theme mounted shows itself. The tests found two things worth recording: Tailwind did not see `mb-5` in `theme.properties`, because the first class of each property was joined to its name (`kcFormGroupClass=mb-5`) and it read the whole as one word; the properties are written with spaces around the sign now, and a test looks for a rule for every class; and `java.net.CookieManager` refuses the cookies of a host with no dot in its name, such as `localhost`, so the test of the real Keycloak keeps the cookies itself. A mutation analysis of the sources of the theme, of the reader of HTML and of the layout (about 430 changes of one token each) left 30 survivors at first: one does nothing (`if-let` for `if-some` over a string or nil) and the others were assertions that were missing (the words of Keycloak's own that the review reads, every void element of HTML, the title of a page, the words that the generator prints), and the checker of accessibility lacked a rule that the survivors showed, that a link inside the page (the skip link) leads to an id the page has; the rule was added, with its test.

## Alternatives considered

- **Tokens written in CSS (`@theme`)**: how Tailwind 4 is meant to be used and the shorter way; the contrast would be checked by eye, and Chart.js, the SVG of the portal and the login would each read the colours in a different way.
- **A token tool such as Style Dictionary**: another Node program for what is a hundred lines of Clojure that the repository already knows how to test.
- **Tailwind without daisyUI**: every component by hand, in three technologies.
- **The play CDN of Tailwind and a CDN stylesheet of daisyUI** for the mock-ups: no build, but nothing a page needs should depend on the network, and the CSS would not be the one that ships.
- **Mock-ups written by hand in HTML**: quicker today and written again in Phase 7. A component in `libs/ui` is written once.
- **The components in `web` and copied to `spa`**, or the reverse: two that drift apart.
- **The design in a tool such as Figma**: outside the repository, and not data.

## Consequences

- There is a Node toolchain in the repository, for development only (Phase 8 needs it anyway, for shadow-cljs).
- Two build steps: Clojure writes `theme.css`, and Tailwind writes `pulse.css` from it and from the sources. Only the first is checked by `bb check`; the second needs Node.
- A class cannot be put together from parts. A component that needs one of several writes them all out: `{:primary "btn-primary" :ghost "btn-ghost"}`.
- The palettes that were not chosen were deleted, and the choice is a commit of its own (teal, 2026-10-16).
- The build of the mock-ups fails when a page uses a class that the compiled stylesheet has no rule for: a misspelling, or a class that was put together from parts, which Tailwind cannot see.
- In both schemes the page is the darker of the two surfaces and a card the lighter (in the dark scheme `base-100` is slate 800 and `base-200` slate 900), so that a card stands out from the page in light and in dark; the contrast test covers both.
- The compiled CSS of the login theme is a build artefact in git, like the realm JSON, and so are its templates, its messages and its icon: they are generated, and changed through `pulse.design.keycloak` and `bb design:keycloak`, never by hand. A change of tokens or of a component needs that task and a commit of what it writes; the tests say so when it is forgotten.
- A change to the English of a page is a change to `messages`, and the review shows it too.
- FreeMarker is a dependency of the tests only. Keycloak ships its own, and the version in `deps.edn` follows the one of Keycloak (26.0.0 ships 2.3.32).
- The login theme does not offer what the realm does not allow, so a realm that turns on being remembered shows the choice without a change to the theme.
- `libs/ui` is what both `web` and `spa` depend on besides `libs/fhir`, and it depends on nothing but Clojure.
